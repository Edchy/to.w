## Design Context

### Users
Primarily people discovering or following the artist's work — friends, potential buyers, anyone drawn to handcraft and jewelry. Not a webshop yet; the site exists to show the work and establish a presence. Browsing is unhurried, personal. The viewer should feel like they've been let into the artist's world, not a storefront.

### Brand Personality
Human, imperfect, down to earth. TO.W makes jewelry and handcraft — physical, tactile, made by hand. The site should feel like it was made with the same care and directness as the objects themselves. Not polished into sterility. Quiet and contemplative, but alive.

Three words: **human · imperfect · grounded**

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** Light and tactile, like printed matter or a working notebook on warm paper.
- **Tone:** Editorial, playful, and handmade. Strong photography and typography establish the structure; drawings interrupt it selectively.
- **Reference:** Independent magazines, archival photo captions, oversized type over grainy imagery, pencil annotations, cobalt sketches, and irregular but deliberate image placement.
- **Anti-references:** Cold jewelry luxury, polished ecommerce grids, generic rustic craft branding, scrapbook clutter, gradient accents, or drawings repeated as decorative stickers.
- **Color:** Warm paper and soft ink, with rare cobalt, faded pink, or marker-yellow interventions.
- **Typography:** Besley for a sturdy printed voice, Bodoni Moda for dramatic poster moments, and Familjen Grotesk for functional text during exploration. Expressive serif faces should not compete as equal voices in one direction.

### Design Principles
1. **Let the work speak.** Images are the content. The UI is the frame, not the painting.
2. **Imperfection is honest.** Preserve the wobble and pressure of real marks. Do not over-smooth or simulate handmade character with generic effects.
3. **Warmth through restraint.** Use generous space and an unhurried rhythm; accents are memorable because they are rare.
4. **Drawings are punctuation.** Sketches, scribbles, and animals should redirect attention or connect ideas, not fill empty space.
5. **Tactile over digital.** Typography, paper tone, grain, and composition should evoke a field notebook, small-run poster, or printed catalogue.
